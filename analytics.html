Analytics

Last updated: October 14, 2026

This page documents what SellerCopilot computes, which Amazon data it reads, and what it gives back. It is written to be checked rather than skimmed, so every report and metric below is named the way Amazon names it.

What the product does

SellerCopilot is a subscription analytics application for Amazon selling partners. It connects to a seller's own selling account through Amazon's Selling Partner API and to the seller's advertising account through the Amazon Ads API. It ingests that account's sales, traffic, advertising, order and financial data every day, refreshes listing and inventory status through the day, and produces a daily brief: a short written account of what changed in the business and what to do about it, with the figures that produced each recommendation attached.

The product covers Amazon Seller Central accounts. A seller authorizes the application to their own selling account and sees only their own data. No customer sees another customer's data, and no customer's analysis is computed from another customer's figures. The SellerCopilot team reviews how accurate recommendations have been across accounts to improve the rules; that review is internal and is never shown to customers.

SellerCopilot reads. It does not create, edit, pause or bid on advertising campaigns, and it does not change prices, listings or inventory. Amazon Ads authorization is granted with Amazon's campaign-management scope; SellerCopilot uses it only to request reports. Recommendations are for the seller to act on.

The daily brief

Amazon's reports settle one to two days behind the calendar, so the brief is anchored to the most recent date the seller's account actually has data for, not to today. The page states that date, so a reader always knows how fresh the analysis is.

Each morning the brief contains four things:

  • Portfolio pulse. Revenue, net profit, ad spend and TACOS for the period, against the preceding one.
  • What's new. Every finding classified against the previous fourteen days: flagged for the first time, resolved since yesterday, persistent for seven days or more with no response, or improving because the metric behind it is moving the right way.
  • Today's actions. The recommendations themselves, ordered by estimated impact, each with an evidence panel holding the readings it was computed from.
  • Recent outcomes. What happened after the seller acted, measured against what was predicted.

How recommendations are generated

Recommendations come from a rule engine, not from a model guessing. Each account is provisioned with twenty-one pre-configured rules. Every rule is a stated condition over the account's own metrics, evaluated for each ASIN once a day, against the seller's targets for TACOS, click-through rate, conversion rate and days of stock. Those targets can be overridden per product, and the rules themselves can be edited, disabled or added to.

The engine produces six kinds of action: stop ads, reduce ads, scale ads, order inventory, fix listing, and win the Buy Box. Real examples of the shipped rules, named as they appear in the product:

  • Stop ads — severe loss + extreme TACOS. The ASIN is losing money per unit and advertising spend is far above its target share of sales.
  • Fix listing — low CVR despite good CTR. Shoppers are clicking at or above target and buying below it, which points at the detail page rather than at the campaign.
  • Scale ads — efficient with room to grow. Spend is converting inside target and stock supports more volume.
  • Order inventory — high demand stockout risk. Seven days of supply or less at the measured sales velocity, on a product that is selling.
  • Listing suppressed by Amazon. The listing is not buyable at all, which no amount of repricing fixes.
  • Nobody holds the Buy Box. No seller owns it, which usually means the price sits above Amazon's threshold.

Each finding is written up with the readings behind it, the estimated impact of acting over the next two weeks, and the contributing factors the rule weighed. A recommendation the seller marks as done, deferred or skipped stops being raised for a set period, so the brief does not repeat itself. Once acted on, the outcome is computed over the following seven days (fourteen for ads being scaled) and compared with the estimate.

Written reports, short explanations of a recommendation or of the monthly scorecard, and an assistant that answers questions about the account's own figures are generated with Anthropic's Claude models. The operations report and the sales dashboard insights replace product names with neutral placeholders before the request is sent and restore them in the response; the executive summary sends only totals. The assistant does not anonymise: to answer questions about specific products it sends product identifiers (ASINs and SKUs) and product titles with the figures. What each feature sends is set out on the security page.

Brand Analytics: what the role is used for

SellerCopilot requests Amazon's Brand Analytics role for one report: GET_SALES_AND_TRAFFIC_REPORT. Amazon lists Brand Analytics as the only role that grants it. SellerCopilot requests it one day at a time, broken down by child ASIN, and requests recent days again as Amazon settles them (see the cadence below). It is the source of the traffic and conversion figures the product is built around.

The fields SellerCopilot reads from it, and what uses each one:

  • sessions and unitsOrdered. Conversion rate is computed from these two (units ordered divided by sessions) for every ASIN. It drives the listing rules, for example Fix listing — low CVR despite good CTR, and the conversion figures on the sales dashboard. Units ordered is also the sales velocity behind the inventory forecast.
  • orderedProductSales. Revenue per ASIN and per day: the starting point of per-product profit, of TACOS, and of the profit- and TACOS-based advertising rules (stop, reduce and scale ads).
  • totalOrderItems. Order counts and average order value on the sales dashboard.
  • buyBoxPercentage. The seller's own Buy Box share over time, weighted by sessions. It drives Buy Box lost — check price and account health and Reduce ads — advertising without the Buy Box, and is how the outcome of a Buy Box recommendation is measured.
  • pageViews. Available to the assistant when a seller asks about page views. No rule uses it.

The report also returns per-device session and page-view breakdowns and unitSessionPercentage. SellerCopilot stores those as Amazon returns them, and no feature uses them today. Its B2B and refund fields are not stored.

Every recommendation built on these figures is computed from the latest ingested day when the seller opens the brief, and its outcome is measured against the same report afterwards, which is why the daily, ASIN-level grain matters.

Profitability per product

Seller Central reports revenue well and profit poorly. It will tell a seller what an ASIN sold. It will not tell them what that ASIN earned after Amazon's fees, the cost of the goods and the advertising that attached to it.

Net profit per ASIN is computed by taking ordered product sales and deducting:

  • FBA fulfilment and referral fees per unit sold, from Amazon's Fee Preview report, which the seller uploads for each marketplace. Each day's sales carry the fee that applied when the day was last ingested.
  • Cost of goods, entered by the seller per product.
  • Sponsored Products spend for the same ASIN over the same period.

Refunds, promotions, storage and other Amazon fees, and Sponsored Brands or Sponsored Display spend are not deducted from this figure. Amazon's financial events, which do carry them, appear on the financial dashboard instead. Without an uploaded Fee Preview report, no Amazon fees are deducted.

The recommendations apply the same deductions over the brief's fourteen-day window, using the FBA and referral fees entered for each listing in the product settings.

The result is a per-product profit figure, plus TACOS measured against the seller's own target rather than a generic benchmark, with ACOS and ROAS reported alongside it. It is presented daily, weekly and monthly, with period-over-period and year-over-year comparison. This is the deliberate difference from a dashboard that reports revenue: an ASIN with growing sales and a negative margin is a problem, and it only looks like one once the fees are in.

Fee Preview estimates are held per marketplace, so fees from one country's Fee Preview are never applied to another country's sales.

How access and data handling work

  • A seller connects SellerCopilot through Amazon's Selling Partner authorization flow, and through Amazon Ads authorization for advertising data.
  • The application reads only the data of the account that authorized it.
  • Authorization can be revoked by the seller at any time from Seller Central, and the advertising authorization from the Amazon account that granted it. After revocation, Amazon no longer returns data to SellerCopilot under that authorization. Revoking does not by itself delete data already stored, and SellerCopilot does not detect a revocation automatically: a seller who revokes access can ask us to delete the account's data.
  • No buyer names, email addresses, phone numbers or street addresses are stored. The repeat-purchase and customer-value analytics need to tell whether two orders came from the same customer. To do that, the first street line of the ship-to address is read in memory, combined with the city, state or province, postal code and country, and turned into a digest (a hash) that is stored in its place; the street line itself is never written down. What is stored with each order line is the digest and the ship-to city, state or province, postal code and country.
  • All traffic is served over HTTPS/TLS. The Amazon credentials that authorize access are encrypted with AES-256-GCM before they are written, with the key held separately from the database. The database is a managed PostgreSQL service that encrypts data at rest, including backups.
  • Data is retained while the account is active. When a subscription is cancelled, a 30-day retention period starts so the account can be resumed. Shortly before it ends, the account's Amazon connections are switched off, and the SellerCopilot team then deletes the account's data. Deletion is carried out by a person, not automatically. A seller can ask for their account and its data to be deleted at any time.
